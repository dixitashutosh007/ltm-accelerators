## 6. Proposal: who re-reads volatile claims every 90 days

**What `scripts/check-claims.cjs` actually enforces.** (1) A claim fails as overdue when its `nextReview` is earlier than the run date. (2) A claim marked `volatile` must have `nextReview` no more than 90 days after `lastReviewed`. (3) `owner` need only be present; it is free text. Nothing checks who edited the dates or that the claim was re-read, and nothing warns before a deadline: the first sign is a red `check:repo`. Today FinOps IQ has 40 volatile claims; the first due date is 12 Jan 2027 (18 claims), so CI turns red on 13 Jan 2027. A Sovereign IQ claim, CLM-SOV-161, is due 16 Nov 2026 and others (CLM-SOV-096, 097, 050, 053, 103, 104) fall due 27 Nov and 4 Dec; CI will fail from 17 Nov unless the sovereignty lead re-reads them.

**Proposed rule (judgement; the owner decides).**
1. A scheduled agent run, once a quarter and no later than 21 days before the earliest `nextReview` for the accelerator, re-reads every claim due in the next 45 days at its primary page, as in this note, and opens a pull request with the register edits and a table like section 4. The agent never merges.
2. The approver is the **product owner**. Make that mechanical: a code-owners entry on `claims-register.json` that requires the product owner's review, so `lastReviewed` can only advance in a reviewed pull request.
3. Keep the `owner` string as it is (`finops-cost-lead (agent); approver: product owner`). It then states the real arrangement: the agent re-reads, the product owner approves. No person is named.
4. If no review has happened 14 days before the date, the scheduled run posts a reminder to the product owner; after the date, the existing check fails the build, as now.
5. Optional hardening, a small change to `check-claims.cjs` for the engineer: print claims due within 30 days as a non-failing warning, so the deadline is visible before CI goes red.

I have not changed `check-claims.cjs`, the schema or any workflow.
